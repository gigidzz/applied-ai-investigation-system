## Design implications

**Chunk size must come down, or chunking does nothing.** At 300 words only 303 of 66,964 records (0.5%) are long enough to split. Comparing two chunking strategies at that size would compare two no-ops. At 160 words 4,965 records (7.4%) split, which is enough for the comparison to mean something. The defaults in `src/ragsys/config.py` are set from this measurement.

**The strategy comparison should be about boundaries, not size.** The median HotpotQA paragraph is 78 words and the median ops record is 24; most records are already a single coherent unit. The informative comparison is therefore *structural* chunking (respect existing paragraph and heading boundaries, split only the long tail) against *fixed* chunking (uniform windows with overlap, ignoring boundaries). Those differ in retrieval granularity even where record lengths are similar.

**Exact-duplicate detection is required, and title identity is not enough.** 18 distinct texts appear more than once across 44 records. The HotpotQA conversion deduplicated by title because the gold labels are title-based, which leaves identical bodies published under different titles. Ingestion must hash content, not just identifiers.

**Date filtering cannot be a hard filter.** ISO dates appear in 97.9% of ops records but only 0.6% of the corpus overall, because HotpotQA paragraphs carry a title and nothing else. A query like "in March" must therefore boost dated records rather than exclude undated ones, or it would discard the entire Wikipedia corpus.

**The long tail is real but small.** The longest record is 1,378 words against a median of 78. Chunking has to handle it correctly, but it will not dominate throughput.
